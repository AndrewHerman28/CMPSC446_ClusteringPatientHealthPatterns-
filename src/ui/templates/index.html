How to read this report

  • Clusters are not risk levels. The clustering models never saw the risk labels. Each patient is placed in a cluster, and the risk level comes from the risk mix of the training patients in that cluster.
  • Confidence is not a calibrated probability. For KMeans, Agglomerative, BIRCH and DBSCAN it is how risk-pure the patient's cluster is. GMM also folds in how certain the cluster membership is.
  • Agreement matters more than any single number. When models disagree, the consensus confidence drops and the case is borderline.
  • DBSCAN may declare a patient an outlier (no dense cluster nearby) and then gives no prediction. It is left out of the consensus.
  • The supervised Random Forest is a reference trained on the true labels, and the only model that outputs real class probabilities.
  • Explanations describe how the models behave, not the patient's health or the cause of any condition.
